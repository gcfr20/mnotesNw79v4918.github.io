Multiplying two two-digit numbers
------------------------------------



.. math::

   & 37 \times 29

   &===================================

   &\text{Multiply unit digits mentally and tabulate: }7\times 9=63

   &\begin{matrix}

    ~ & 3 \\

    6 & ~
    \end{matrix}

   &===================================
    
    &\text{Cross-multiply the two digits mentally and tablulate: }3\times 9+7\times 2=41

    &\begin{matrix}

    ~ & 1 & 3 \\

    4 & 6 & ~
    \end{matrix}

    &===================================`

    &\text{Multiply tens digits mentally and tablulate: }3\times 2=6

    &\begin{matrix}

    6 & 1 & 3 \\

    4 & 6 & ~
    \end{matrix}

   &===================================
    
    &\text{Do the addition (with any carry-overs) to get the result:}


    & 1073

Multiplying degree 1 polynomials
-----------------------------------

Note the following algebraic manipulation:

.. math::

   (ax+b)(cx+d) = ax(cx+d)+b(cx+d) = acx^2+adx+bcx+bd
   =(ac)x^2+(ad+bc)x+bd
   
Following this pattern:

.. math::

   (2x+1)(3x+5)=(2\times 3)x^2+(2\times 5+1\times 3)x+(1\times 5)=6x^2+13x+5

Sign has to be preserved with the coefficient

.. math::

   (2x-1)(3x+5)=(2\times 3)x^2+[2\times 5 +(-1)\times 3]x+(-1)\times 5=6x^2+7x-5

Many times the polynomials are monic (leading coefficient is 1). In that case, we have

.. math::

   (x+b)(x+c)=x^2+(b+c)x+bc

We just need the sum of the constants (with sign preserved) and the product of the constants.

.. math::

   (x+2)(x-3)=x^2+[2+(-3)]x+(2)(-3)=x^2-x-6
