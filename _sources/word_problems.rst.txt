
Word Problems
======================================


One variable equations
--------------------------

Qiang drives 15 miles at an average speed of 30 miles per hour. How many additional miles will he have to drive at 55 miles per hour to average 50 miles per hour for the entire trip?

.. math::

   & x: \text{Additional miles to be driven}

   & \text{Total distance} = 15 + x

   & \text{Total time} = \frac{15}{30}+\frac{x}{55}

   & \text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}}

   & \implies 50 = \frac{15+x}{\frac{15}{30}+\frac{x}{55}} = \frac{15+x}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{x}{55}} 

   & 50\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{x}{55}\right) = 15 + x

   & 25 + \frac{10x}{11} = 15 + x

   & 10 + \frac{10x}{11} = x

   & 110 + 10x = 11 x

   & x = 110

======================================

On a trip to the beach, Anh traveled 50 miles on the highway and 10 miles on a coastal access road. He drove three times as fast on the highway as on the coastal road. If Anh spent 30 minutes driving on the coastal road, how many minutes did his entire trip take?

.. math::

   & x: \text{Total time}

   & \text{Time on highway} = x - 30

   & \text{Speed on highway} = 3 \times \text{speed on coastal road}
   
   &\frac{50}{x-30}=3 \times \frac{10}{30} = 1

   & x = 50 + 30 = 80

======================================

Jeremy's father drives him to school in rush hour traffic in 20 minutes. One day, there is no traffic, so his father can drive him 18 miles per hour faster and gets him to school in 12 minutes. How far in miles is it to school?

.. math::

   & x:~\text{Speed in rush hour}

   & \text{Distance to school} = 20 \times x = 12 \times \left(x + \frac{18}{60}\right)

   & \implies 5x=3\left(x+\frac{3}{10}\right)

   & \implies 2x=\frac{9}{10}

   & \text{Distance to school} = 20\times x = 10\times \frac{9}{10}=9~\text{miles}

======================================

Between 3 and an unknown number is a third number such that the three numbers form an arithmetic progression. If the middle number is reduced by 6, the numbers form a geometric progression. Find the unknown number.


Note: If :math:`a,~b,~c` are in arithmetic progression, then :math:`b=\frac{a+c}{2}` (the arithmetic mean). If :math:`a,~b,~c` are in geometric progression, then :math:`b=\sqrt{ac}` (the geometric mean).

.. math::

   & d: \text{The common difference for the arithmetic progression}

   & \text{Arithmetic progression: }\text{3,~3+d,~3+2d}

   & \text{Geometric progression: }\text{3,~3+d-6=d-3,~3+2d}

   & \implies (d-3)^2=3(3+2d)

   & d^2-6d+9=9+6d \implies d^2 = 12d \implies d = 12

   & \text{Unknown number }=3+2d=27

======================================

The clock in Sri's car, which is not accurate, gains time at a constant rate. One day as he begins shopping, he notes that his car clock and his watch (which is accurate) both say 12:00 noon. When he is done shopping, his watch says 12:30 and his car clock says 12:35. Later that day, Sri loses his watch. He looks at his car clock and it says 7:00. What is the actual time?

.. math::

   & x: \text{Actual time since noon in minutes}

   & \text{7 hours = 420 minutes}

   & \text{Rate of time gain by the car clock} = \frac{420-x}{x}=\frac{35-30}{30}

   & \frac{420-x}{x}=\frac{1}{6}\implies x=6\times 420 - 6x\implies x=360

   & \text{Answer: 6:00}

======================================

Each day for four days, Linda traveled for one hour at a speed that resulted in her traveling one mile in an integer number of minutes. Each day after the first, her speed decreased so that the number of minutes to travel one mile increased by 5 minutes over the preceding day. Each of the four days, her distance traveled was also an integer number of miles. What was the total number of miles for the four trips?

.. math::

   & m:~\text{Number of minutes needed for a mile on day 1}

   & \text{Total miles} = \frac{60}{m} + \frac{60}{m+5} + \frac{60}{m+10} + \frac{60}{m+15}

   & \text{Thus, $m$, $m+5$, $m+10$, and $m+15$ are divisors of $60$}

   & \implies m = 5

   & \text{Total miles} = \frac{60}{5} + \frac{60}{10} + \frac{60}{15} + \frac{60}{20} = 12 + 6 + 4 + 3 = 25

======================================
   
George walks 1 mile to school. He leaves home at the same time each day, walks at a steady speed of 3 miles per hour, and arrives just as school begins. Today he was distracted by the pleasant weather and walked the first 1/2 mile at a speed of only 2 miles per hour. At how many miles per hour must George run the last 1/2 mile in order to arrive just as school begins today?

.. math::

   & v:~\text{Speed at which George must run the last 1/2 mile today}

   & \text{Time to school has to be the same as on a regular day}

   & \text{Time to school} = \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1/2}{2} + \frac{1/2}{v}

   & \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2v}

   & \frac{1}{3} = \frac{v+2}{4v}

   & 4v = 3v + 6 \implies v = 6~\text{miles per hour}

======================================

In a class of 10 students, the average score on a test was 75. However, the teacher realized that one of the students accidentally received 100 instead of 50 on the test. If the mistake is corrected, what will be the correct average score?

.. math::

   & x:~\text{Correct average score}

   & \text{Total score before correction - Total score after correction}= 50

   & 10\times 75 - 10\times x = 50

   & x = 75 -\frac{50}{10} =  70


Two variable equations
--------------------------

Starting with some gold coins and some empty treasure chests, I tried to put 9 gold coins in each treasure chest, but that left 2 treasure chests empty. So instead I put 6 gold coins in each treasure chest, but then I had 3 gold coins left over. How many gold coins did I have?

.. math::

   & x: \text{Number of coins}

   & y: \text{Number of chests}

   & 9(y-2) = x, 6y+3 = x \implies 9(y-2)=6y+3 \implies 3(y-2)=2y+1\implies y=7

   & \implies x = 6y+3 = 45

   
Recursive equations
---------------------------

Gilda has a bag of marbles. She gives 20% of them to her friend Pedro. Then Gilda gives 10% of what is left to another friend, Ebony. Finally, Gilda gives 25%$ of what is now left in the bag to her brother Jimmy. What percentage of her original bag of marbles does Gilda have left for herself?

.. math::

   & x: \text{Initial count}, r_k: \text{Marbles remaining after step }k
   
   & r_1 = (1-0.2)x = \frac{4}{5}x

   & r_2 = (1-0.1)r_1 = \frac{9}{10}r_1

   & r_3 = (1-0.25)r_2 = \frac{3}{4}r_2

   & r_3 = \frac{3\times 9\times 4}{4\times 10\times 5}x =\frac{27}{50}x

   & \text{Answer: }54\%

======================================

Hui is an avid reader. She bought a copy of the best seller Math is Beautiful. On the first day, Hui read 1/5 of the pages plus 12 more, and on the second day she read 1/4 of the remaining pages plus 15 pages. On the third day she read 1/3 of the remaining pages plus 18 pages. She then realized that there were only 62 pages left to read, which she read the next day. How many pages are in this book?

.. math::

   & r_1 = x - \left(\frac{x}{5}+12\right) = \frac{4x}{5}-12

   & r_2 = r_1 - \left(\frac{r_1}{4}+15\right) = \frac{3r_1}{4}-15

   & r_3 = r_2 - \left(\frac{r_2}{3}+18\right) = \frac{2r_2}{3}-18

   & r_3 = 62 \implies r_2 = \frac{3(62+18)}{2}=3\times 40 = 120

   & \implies r_1 = \frac{4(120+15)}{3}=4\times 45 = 180

   & \implies x = \frac{5(180+12)}{4}=5\times 48 = 10\times 24 = 240


Easy equations that look daunting
-----------------------------------

The equation :math:`(x^2-3)^2-4(x^2-3)+3=0` looks daunting. However, setting :math:`y=x^2-3` gives us

.. math::

   & y^2-4y+3=0

that results in :math:`y=3` and :math:`y=1`

Further, :math:`y=x^2-3\implies 3=x^2-3~\text{and}~1=x^2-3`. Both equations can be solved easily.
